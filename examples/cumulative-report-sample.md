## Executive Summary

Across 19 weeks of customer feedback, AI Cost Analyzer has established a clear primary value proposition: customers consistently trust the platform most when it finds stale, unused, or orphaned cloud resources they were not catching on their own. That signal showed up across 28 of 32 meetings and cut across every vertical in the data set, from banks and healthcare systems to retailers and manufacturing teams. When customers describe early wins, they almost always start with discovered waste, avoided spend, or reduced security exposure from stale assets.

The strongest expansion blocker is not detection quality; it is organizational attribution. Once initial value is proven, customers immediately want to explain savings by department, cost center, business unit, or application portfolio. That requirement spans finance, engineering, and executive stakeholders and has become effectively non-negotiable for broader rollout. Customers are not asking for more raw recommendations first; they are asking for the ability to connect those recommendations to ownership, accountability, and leadership reporting.

Deletion-related trust must also be earned before customers will operationalize the product. Regulated customers need human approval and audit trails, while more operationally mature customers want impact previews and higher-confidence scoring before a resource is labeled safe to delete. Bugs that inflate savings or mislabel active resources have outsized impact because they undermine the credibility of the larger recommendation set. Across the historical data, the pattern is consistent: customers will tolerate missing automation longer than they will tolerate untrustworthy automation.

Finally, cost visibility is increasingly cross-functional. Finance teams want department rollups, executives want polished reports without product login, security teams care about stale identities and attack surface, and operations teams need smarter thresholds to avoid noise. The product is resonating, but long-term adoption depends on evolving from a detection engine into a trusted operating layer for cloud savings decisions.
